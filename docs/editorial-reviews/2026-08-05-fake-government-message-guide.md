## Material correction

The draft broadly grouped PIB Fact Check and the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal together. The reviewed version separates four different routes:

1. **PIB Fact Check** for suspicious information concerning the Government of India, its ministries, departments and public-sector entities.
2. **I4C Report Suspect** for attempted cybercrime identifiers such as suspicious URLs, phone numbers, WhatsApp/Telegram handles, email IDs, SMS headers and social URLs when the person has not yet become a victim.
3. **Chakshu / Sanchar Saathi** for suspected fraudulent calls, SMS and WhatsApp communications.
4. **NCRP and 1930** for victims, with immediate 1930 reporting for cyber financial fraud.

The article now states that the I4C suspect repository is complaint-based, incomplete and not an authenticity certificate. A missing result does not establish that a website or app is safe.

## Claims deliberately bounded

- PIB Fact Check is not described as a universal fact-checking or police service.
- Filing a PIB query does not guarantee a public verdict or compensation.
- Reporting through I4C, Chakshu, NCRP or 1930 does not guarantee recovery, blocking or prosecution.
- A `.gov.in` suffix, HTTPS padlock, logo, letterhead or privacy policy is not treated as sufficient proof by itself.
- No public identifier is labelled criminal without a competent authority or completed legal finding.
- The guide does not provide individual legal, banking, forensic or malware-removal advice.
