UDISE+ 2025–26 records **4,133 zero-enrolment schools** in West Bengal with **19,502 teachers** associated with them. The same state-wise data records **6,769 single-teacher schools** with a combined enrolment of **229,569 students**.

These figures raise a significant question about how school and teacher resources are distributed across the state. They should, however, be read as administrative data points rather than as proof that every zero-enrolment school is closed or physically empty.

## Key facts

| --- | ---: |
| Total schools | 93,147 |
| Total enrolment | 17,726,591 |
| Total teachers | 581,774 |
| Pupil-Teacher Ratio | 30 |
| Zero-enrolment schools | 4,133 |
| Teachers recorded in those schools | 19,502 |
| Single-teacher schools | 6,769 |

## What the UDISE+ data shows

The Ministry of Education's Unified District Information System for Education Plus, or **UDISE+ 2025–26**, provides state-wise statistics for schools, enrolment and teachers.

For West Bengal, Table 2.2 records **93,147 schools**, **17,726,591 enrolments** and **581,774 teachers**, with a pupil-teacher ratio of **30**.

The same table records **4,133 zero-enrolment schools** and **19,502 teachers** associated with those schools.

It also records **6,769 single-teacher schools** with **229,569 enrolments**.

## The 6,769 single-teacher-school figure also appears in Parliament

A Ministry of Education reply to the Rajya Sabha dated **29 July 2026** independently lists **6,769 single-teacher schools in West Bengal** for 2025–26.

The annexure also records **100,843 single-teacher schools across India** and identifies UDISE+ 2025–26 as its source.

That provides a separate current official confirmation of the West Bengal single-teacher-school count.

## What “zero enrolment” does — and does not — establish

The wording matters.

A school recorded with **zero enrolment in UDISE+** should not automatically be described as a school that is closed, physically empty every day, or employing teachers who perform no work.

UDISE+ is an administrative education-data system. Establishing the operational position of an individual school would require school-level records or a separate finding by the responsible authority.

The state-level data therefore supports the enrolment and staffing numbers. It does not, by itself, establish why individual schools have those figures.

## Why the figures matter

The two findings point to a distribution question.

Thousands of schools are recorded with zero enrolment while thousands of teachers are associated with those institutions. At the same time, **6,769 schools are recorded as single-teacher schools**, despite collectively serving more than **229,000 enrolled students**.

The UDISE+ table does not itself explain the cause of this pattern. School location, enrolment updates, transfers, administrative status, restructuring or other factors would require separate official evidence before being presented as explanations.

## What readers should not infer from the table

The state-level figures do not establish that:

- any particular teacher is responsible for the situation;
- all 4,133 institutions are formally closed;
- every zero-enrolment entry has the same cause;
- transferring a stated number of teachers would automatically resolve staffing shortages; or
- UDISE+ figures can be combined with audit findings from a different period without explaining differences in scope and methodology.

## How to check the official data

Open the official **UDISE+ publications** page and locate **“UDISE+ Report 2025-26 – Existing Structure.”** The relevant state-wise figures are in **Table 2.2**.

The single-teacher-school figure can also be checked against the Ministry of Education's **Rajya Sabha reply dated 29 July 2026**.
