Government schemes, jobs, certificates, complaints and public services are spread across many websites. Fraudulent sites can copy official names, logos and layouts, so the first search result is not automatically safe for personal information, documents or payments.

This guide gives a repeatable method for finding and checking genuine government websites and service links. **Citizen Affairs is not a government website.** Always re-check the responsible authority's current instructions before applying or making a transaction.

## 1. Start with the National Portal of India

[India.gov.in](https://www.india.gov.in/) is the National Portal of India and is managed by the National Informatics Centre. It provides access to information about Central and State governments, directories, schemes, services and public utilities.

When you know the name of a ministry, department or service, search for it on India.gov.in first. Follow the portal's directory entry to the responsible authority's main website, and then use that website's notice, recruitment, service or contact section.

## 2. Verify departments through the Web Directory

The [India.gov.in Web Directory](https://www.india.gov.in/directory/web-directory) helps users find websites for Union ministries and departments, States and Union Territories, districts, the judiciary, legislatures and Indian missions.

A safe process is:

1. Open the Web Directory.
2. Select the relevant Union, State/UT, district, judiciary or other section.
3. Find the authority or department.
4. Open the website listed in the directory.
5. Navigate from that main site to the required notice, recruitment or service page.

Do not rely only on a forwarded deep link. Opening the main official website first gives you an additional cross-check.

## 3. Use the Integrated Government Online Directory

The [Integrated Government Online Directory—IGOD](https://igod.gov.in/) is a directory of Indian government websites maintained by the National Informatics Centre. It covers Central, State, district, judiciary, legislature, mission and sector-based entries.

If a website is difficult to find through India.gov.in, search IGOD by organisation name or website title. A directory listing is a strong starting signal, but important applications and payments should still be opened through the responsible department's main homepage.

## 4. Find online services through India.gov.in

The older `services.india.gov.in` address now leads to the [Services section of India.gov.in](https://www.india.gov.in/services). Use the current Services page as the discovery point.

After opening a service listing, check:

- which department or authority operates it;
- whether it is a Central or State service;
- where eligibility and document requirements are published;
- whether the application or payment link is connected from the authority's official website;
- whether the helpline and grievance details match the department's contact page.

A legitimate service can use a separate authorised portal, but the handoff should be traceable from the responsible authority.

## 5. Read the complete domain name

Before entering personal information, inspect the address bar carefully:

- check spelling and extra hyphens;
- look for unnecessary words before or after the organisation name;
- identify the actual registered domain, not just a familiar word in the path;
- remember that `https` and a padlock do not prove government ownership;
- avoid shortened links from messages and social media;
- compare the domain with the official directory and department homepage.

For example, a domain containing `gov`, `india` or the name of a ministry is not automatically official.

## 6. Verify the issuing authority and notice

A genuine recruitment notice, scheme update or service announcement should normally identify the issuing authority. Check:

- notice or reference number, where applicable;
- issue date;
- name and designation of the issuing office;
- official contact details;
- a matching copy or announcement on the authority's main website;
- whether the application dates and fees agree across the notice and portal.

Do not trust only a PDF logo, letterhead, QR code or screenshot. These can be copied or altered.

## 7. Check contact and payment details

Use contact information published on the official website, not a phone number supplied only in a message. Be cautious when a service asks for:

- payment to a personal UPI ID or private bank account;
- OTP, PIN, CVV or internet-banking password;
- installation of an unknown APK or remote-access app;
- document submission through WhatsApp or Telegram;
- urgent payment without an official notice or receipt.

Government fees, where applicable, should follow the payment route described by the responsible authority and should produce an acknowledgement or receipt.

## 8. What to do when directories disagree or an entry is missing

Government websites can move, merge or change. When an entry is missing or two links differ:

1. Search both India.gov.in and IGOD.
2. Check the organisation's verified parent ministry or State portal.
3. Compare contact details and recent official notices.
4. Use the official helpline to confirm the current portal.
5. Do not submit information until the destination is clear.

A broken or missing directory link does not prove that a service is fake, but it means you need another official cross-check.

## Final safety checklist

Before using a government website, confirm that:

- the organisation is listed in an official directory or linked by its parent authority;
- the full domain is correctly spelled;
- the issuing authority and current notice are identifiable;
- contact and grievance details match the official site;
- the application and payment route is cross-linked from the main website;
- no one is requesting passwords, OTPs or payment to a personal account.

Use official directories for discovery, and use the responsible authority's current website as the final source for rules, applications and payments.
